# The product ends with a direction, never a verdict

The product exists because its Users lack information about the day-to-day
reality of jobs, so it must end with something concrete rather than handing
back an evidence map and wishing them luck. But naming a best career would
make us a personality quiz with better manners: users read the shape of an
output, not the epistemology behind it, and "your preferences lean toward
Product Manager" is heard as "you scored 82% Product Manager" no matter how
carefully it was derived.

We therefore distinguish two things a final screen could say. A **verdict**
answers "which career should I pick?" — we refuse this, and we could not earn
it from one work sample anyway. A **direction** answers "what should I
explore FIRST?" — a prioritised next action, which is what we give.

Consequences: a Career Option's name may appear in the output only as
evidence the User reasons from ("you asked for more Analysis, which is Core
to Management Consulting"), never as a conclusion we hand them. This
preserves `AGENTS.md` principle 1 while delivering the concreteness that
differentiates us from a careers counsellor, whose advice our Users find
ambiguous. Every screen in Phase 3 depends on this framing.
