# A Contradiction is surfaced and questioned, never overwritten

When a User asks for more of an Activity Group in Phase 2 and then wants the
same or less after doing it, we show them the Contradiction and ask one
question: was it this kind of work, or was it something about how this
particular task was set up?

We do not silently treat the Informed Preference as correct and discard the
Imagined one. One task is thin evidence, and overwriting what the User told us
would strip them of exactly the judgement the product exists to build. Nor do
we merely display the Contradiction and move on: "I dislike analysis" and "I
disliked *that* analysis task" are entirely different findings that look
identical without asking, and a badly authored experiment would otherwise be
recorded as a fact about the User.

Where a Contradiction sits on a high-ranking Unknown, "test this again in a
real setting" becomes the Direction.

Constraint: one question, not a battery. Each additional prompt after the
experiment costs User attention, and a reflection screen that becomes a form
loses the quality that separates this from a quiz. Prefer fewer, better
questions and accept a less complete picture.
